📷 Light Meter Calibration Guide

Use this guide to verify and calibrate your light meter against your phone's camera metering.

How to Test Accuracy

  1. Open Your Phone's Camera App in manual/pro mode (if available)
  2. Set the Same ISO in both apps (e.g., ISO 400)
  3. Point at the Same Scene - aim both at a gray card or neutral scene
  4. Compare EV Values:
    • Light Meter displays EV directly
    • Phone camera shows aperture + shutter speed
    • Calculate EV from phone: EV = log₂(f²/t)

EV Calculation Formula

EV = log₂(N² / t) Where: N = f-number (aperture) t = shutter speed in seconds Example: f/2.8 @ 1/125s EV = log₂(2.8² / (1/125)) EV = log₂(2.8² × 125) EV = log₂(980) EV ≈ 9.9

Metering Modes Explained

Spot Metering

Measures: Small center circle (10% of frame)

Best For: Precise metering of specific subjects, high contrast scenes, backlighting

Use When: You want to expose for a specific part of the scene (face, sky, etc.)

Most accurate for matching what you see in the viewfinder center

Ambient Metering

Measures: Entire frame with center-weighted average

Best For: General scenes, balanced lighting, landscapes

Use When: The whole scene matters, not just the center

Similar to evaluative/matrix metering in cameras

Common Differences & Why

±0.5 EV difference: Normal variation, within acceptable range

±1.0 EV difference: May be due to:

  • Different metering areas (spot vs matrix)
  • Camera applying exposure compensation automatically
  • Different color temperature handling
  • Sensor sensitivity differences

±2.0 EV or more: Requires calibration adjustment

Calibration Adjustment

If readings are consistently off:

  1. Test in 3-5 different lighting conditions
  2. Calculate average difference (e.g., -1.2 EV)
  3. Open app.js in a text editor
  4. Find the line: const K = 12.5;
  5. Adjust K value:
    • Readings too high? Increase K (try 14, 16, 18)
    • Readings too low? Decrease K (try 11, 10, 9)
  6. Test again and repeat until accurate
⚠️ Important Notes:
  • Phone cameras often apply automatic exposure compensation
  • HDR mode on phones can affect metering readings
  • Test with HDR/AI features DISABLED for accurate comparison
  • Use manual/pro mode if available
  • Gray card or 18% gray reference is most accurate for testing

Quick Verification Method

Sunny 16 Rule Test:

  1. Go outside on a bright sunny day
  2. Point both apps at a sunlit scene
  3. Set ISO 100
  4. Light meter should read approximately EV 15-16
  5. Phone camera should suggest f/16 @ 1/100s (or equivalent)

If both agree within ±1 EV, your calibration is good!

Need More Help?

Still getting inconsistent readings? Consider these factors:

  • Scene reflectance: Very dark or very bright subjects can fool meters
  • White balance: Can affect luminance calculations
  • Lens quality: Phone camera lens coatings affect light transmission
  • Sensor differences: Each phone sensor responds slightly differently

For critical work, a dedicated handheld light meter is still the gold standard. This app is designed for general photography and learning exposure principles.
